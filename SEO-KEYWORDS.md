## How to use and measure this map

The UE competitor and website brief's keyword bank contains 133 English, 36 Greek and 32 Russian Keyword Planner records. Its Limassol and Cyprus estimates cover different areas and rounded close variants can overlap. The long-tail questions above are editorial proposals derived from those themes, local provider terminology and UE’s own session descriptions. Their individual search volumes, rankings and conversion rates have not been measured.

- Give the home page the broad studio-discovery query. Send an interview technique or recording query to the podcast page and a price or hourly-hire query to the rates page. Place the question where the page can answer it with the session steps, handoff, or proposed pricing.
- For YouTube, write about a physical shoot. Queries for the YouTube Studio login and analytics dashboard belong to Google’s software. Property content concerns studio-based agent interviews and buyer guides. DJ content concerns filming performers at UE Studios. Terms for drone surveys, location shoots, wedding DJ bookings and sound-equipment hire imply different offers.
- Keep the show discoverable under its own name. Its local interview format is in development. The planner’s conversion is a downloaded brief. Count an actual enquiry or reservation separately once that workflow exists.
- In public-launch QA, open each sitemap URL and verify its status, indexability, matching language, self-canonical and reciprocal alternates. Keep a separate English fallback and check that a switch from a service or sector lands on its equivalent. In private review, keep indexing disabled.
- After launch, compare Search Console impressions, clicks and click-through rate for each exact landing page and query in Cyprus over the first 28 days, then compare the next 28 days. Use the page language and actual wording of queries to interpret intent. Country alone cannot identify a visitor’s preferred language. If two pages alternate for the same query, check whether the results imply a service, a rate or a branded visit before revising titles or internal links.
